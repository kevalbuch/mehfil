PRODUCT BRIEF FOR CLAUDE
India Sound Culture Engine
A detailed product and build plan for a creator-first platform that helps Gen Z
discover, understand, and use the sounds moving across India.
Not what India is listening to.
What India is creating with.
Working name: Mehfil. The name is provisional; the product direction is not.
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The opportunity
India's short-form culture moves through sound. A Punjabi independent release can break through Chandigarh
college edits, a Tamil hook can travel from dance creators into Bengaluru transitions, and an old Hindi song
can return through wedding or nostalgia reels. Today, discovery is fragmented: users hear a sound, but
struggle to identify it, judge whether it is still timely, or turn it into a post idea.
Mehfil is a creator utility and cultural discovery layer. It recommends the right sound for a user's exact content
moment, gives the best hook and a fresh concept, and explains whether the sound is early, rising, peaking,
saturated, or returning as a retro comeback.
1. Product thesis
The product must not become another streaming service or generic Top 50 chart. Its job is to shorten the
journey from "I want to post" to "I have the right sound and a stronger idea."
Primary promise
Find the sound before it becomes everyone's sound.
Mehfil should make emerging sound culture legible across languages and scenes - Hindi, Punjabi, Tamil,
Telugu, Malayalam, Marathi, Bengali, Gujarati, Kannada, Haryanvi, Assamese, Indian indie, and more. No
language is framed as secondary; every scene can be the source of the next national moment.

3. Core experience
The homepage begins with a question, not a chart:
What are you posting today?
Users select a moment: outfit reveal, travel dump, soft launch, wedding, college farewell, birthday,
funny/meme, gym, breakup, or a custom mood. The system returns three sound cards. Each is practical,
culturally rich, and immediately usable.

Song + artist + language Proper credit and a clear entry into India-wide discovery.

4. Key user flows
A. Post Match - the daily utility
Open Mehfil -> choose what you are posting -> choose a mood or add a few details -> receive three sounds ->
compare hook, trend stage, and idea -> save or open a chosen sound -> post. This should take under one
minute.
B. Catch the Sound - the discovery utility
Search an imperfect lyric, song title, artist, language, use case, or vibe such as "Punjabi song for bike edit" or
"rainy Tamil love song" -> see likely matches -> open the sound story -> save it to a themed board.
C. Sound Route - the cultural utility
Open a track -> see how it is moving: origin scene, cities or communities where it picked up, reel formats
adopting it, and whether it is expanding nationally. This turns trend data into a story users want to share.
D. The Vault - the retention utility
Users save tracks to personal boards such as "my future wedding", "songs for train journeys", "college will end
someday", or "my 2026 sound." These collections are more emotionally useful than a generic liked-songs list.

6. Trend intelligence approach
The early product should not claim perfect real-time, platform-wide data. It should earn trust through
transparent editorial curation and clear labels. Launch with a compact catalog of 30-50 active sounds, reviewed
daily or several times per week.
Signals to combine
- Momentum: how quickly examples, saves, search interest, or creator submissions are increasing.
- Format fit: the kind of post where the sound is actually working.
- Geography and scene: city, campus, language scene, wedding culture, dance community, or creator niche.

- Freshness: whether there is still creative room for a new creator.
- Editorial judgment: a human reviewer keeps the catalog culturally sharp and prevents low-quality trend
noise.
Recommended labels
Early - visible inside a niche but not broadly copied. Rising - gaining momentum and worth using now.
Peaking - high reach but needs a fresh execution. Saturated - widely overused; show alternatives. Retro
comeback - an older track resurfacing through new reel behavior.
7. Data, platform, and music-rights rules
The product must be designed around platform and licensing reality, not around brittle scraping. Do not
promise that every Instagram trend metric can be accessed programmatically. Start with approved and
observable sources, editorial review, creator submissions, and future data or label partnerships.
- Do not host full tracks without the required rights and licensing.
- Use official outbound destinations for playback where available, and clearly credit track, artist, language, film,
label, and source.
- Use short previews only when a permitted source or license allows them.
- Treat an Instagram Reel link import as a future feature contingent on supported access and policy review.
- Give users a way to report incorrect credit, inappropriate categorization, or stale trend information.
Meta has previously described an in-app Reels trends destination showing trending audio, usage count, topics, and hashtags. That
validates the user need, but our value is the creator decision layer: context, freshness, alternatives, and concepts. Source: Meta
newsroom, "New Features on Instagram Reels: Trends, Editing and Gifts," April 2023.
8. Content design and tone
The design should feel like a saved reel memory and a modern Indian music magazine: warm, editorial, calm,
and tactile. Avoid a loud analytics dashboard or a generic streaming-library interface.
- Use one featured sound or moment at a time, with film-grain-inspired texture and restrained typography.
- Show utility in plain language: "use this now," "best for a photo dump," "too saturated - try this instead."
- Represent regions and languages equally through rich context, never through a hierarchy that treats one as
the default.
- Build sharing around taste: "I found this before it peaked," "your city is looping this," and "my 2026 sound."

10. Success metrics
- Activation: percentage of new users who complete Post Match and save or open a sound.
- Time to value: median time from landing to a usable recommendation.
- Recommendation quality: save rate, outbound click rate, and direct user feedback on match relevance.
- Retention: weekly return rate for users who saved at least one sound board.
- Catalog health: percentage of active sounds that represent independent and non-Hindi scenes.
- Creator impact: later, users reporting that they posted with a Mehfil-recommended sound.
11. Decisions Claude should preserve
- Do not design this as a Spotify or YouTube clone.
- The primary interaction is moment-first recommendation, not browsing an infinite chart.
- Every sound recommendation needs a hook, trend stage, use case, and fresh idea.
- India-wide inclusion is foundational, not a later localization layer.
- Start curated and trustworthy; automate only after there is a clear source strategy.
- Respect music rights and platform policies from day one.
- Keep the user experience calm, editorial, and emotionally resonant - never data-heavy or overwhelming.
